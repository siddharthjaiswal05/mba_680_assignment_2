LayeredMBA680 robo-advisory prototype

Build me a portfolio

Eight short sections. They measure how you trade off risk, how you trade off time, how you feel about losses as against gains, how you read probabilities, and where you sit on the five personality domains. At the end the portal fits your utility and value functions and builds you two portfolios: the one expected utility theory says you should hold, and the one behavioral portfolio theory says you will actually hold. Nothing leaves this device until you export it.

01

About you

The corpus and the horizon set the scale for everything that follows.

02

Risk ladder

Each row is the same offer: leave your corpus untouched, or take a coin flip that loses 10% of it on tails and gains the listed amount on heads. Work down the rows and pick the first one you would accept. The row you switch at brackets your relative risk aversion.

#Heads, you gainTails, you loseYour choice

If you would refuse every row, pick refuse on all ten.

03

Time preference

Two questions. The gap between them separates impatience you feel only about today from the rate at which you discount the future generally.

You can have ₹10,000 today, or a larger amount in one month.
What is the smallest amount in one month that would make you wait?
₹
You can have ₹10,000 in twelve months, or a larger amount in thirteen months.
What is the smallest amount in thirteen months that would make you wait the extra month?

04

Personality

Thirty statements. Say how much each one describes you, from 1 (disagree strongly) to 5 (agree strongly). Answer quickly; first reactions score better than considered ones.

05

Your utility function

Five questions, all the same shape. You are offered a certain amount, or a gamble between the best and worst outcomes on the table. Move the slider to the chance of the best outcome that leaves you exactly indifferent. That probability is your utility for the certain amount, which is what lets the portal draw the curve rather than assume it.

06

Your value function

The same method, but now measured as gains and losses from where you stand today rather than as levels of wealth. This is the part that separates prospect theory from expected utility theory.

A coin flip: tails you lose ₹10,000, heads you win G.
What is the smallest G at which you would take the bet?
₹
07

How you read probabilities

Two certainty equivalents. Together they reveal whether you overweight long shots and underweight near-certainties, which almost everyone does.

08

Floors, income and aspiration

Behavioral portfolio theory needs to know what you are protecting and what you are reaching for. These are the numbers that build the layers.

Split 100 points between two things.
Never falling below your floor, against having a real shot at your aspiration.
How much expected end wealth would you give up to raise your chance of hitting the aspiration by ten percentage points?
Zero is a perfectly reasonable answer.
₹
